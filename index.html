The 6 Core Foundations

1. Your Parenting Legacy

Children do what we do, not what we say. Unpack your childhood triggers and inner critic so you don't project past wounds onto your child.

Reflective Pause: When your child triggers strong anger, ask: "Does this feeling belong to the present situation, or am I reacting to what I felt at their age?"

2. Your Child's Environment

Family structure matters less than how family members treat each other. Avoid Fact Tennis A defensive style of arguing where partners lob logical arguments back and forth to 'win' rather than listening to underlying emotions. and hostile arguments that threaten your child's safety.

I-Statements Expressing your own feelings ("I feel overwhelmed") instead of defining or blaming the other person ("You are annoying"). : Use statements like "I feel hurt when I am ignored" instead of blaming with "You always ignore me".

3. Feelings & Containment

All feelings are valid; not all behaviors are acceptable. Be a calm container for your child's emotional distress rather than distracting or dismissing them.

Felt With, Not Dealt With Sitting alongside a child to empathize with their painful mood instead of rushing to 'fix' or dismiss them. : When a child is sad or angry, stay alongside them: "It is hard when things don't go your way. I'm right here with you."

4. Laying a Foundation

Parenting begins before birth. Secure attachment develops through attunement, responding to Coercive Cries A baby's biologically programmed alarm cry that compels caregivers to respond to their essential needs. , and respectful Sleep Nudging Gradually encouraging independent sleep step-by-step from a child's comfort baseline rather than leaving them to cry alone. .

Gentle Separation: Move step-by-step from your child's comfort baseline rather than leaving them to cry alone in distress.

5. Conditions for Good Mental Health

Mental health thrives on reciprocal, turn-taking dialogue. Avoid phone addiction, practice engaged observation, and allow uninterrupted play.

Leave a Space: Notice your child's cues and leave pauses in conversation or play so they can take their turn and influence you.

6. Behavior is Communication

All inconvenient behavior is an unrefined signal. Define your own boundaries, engage in collaborative problem-solving, and handle lies with curiosity.

Set Boundaries Clearly: Say "I cannot allow you to jump on the sofa" instead of judging with "You are being bad".
